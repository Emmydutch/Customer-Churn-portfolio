## Title

Predicting Telecom Customer Churn and Optimising Retention Decisions: An Explainable and Prescriptive Analytics Approach

## Author

Emmanuel Onuoha

## Work type

Independent technical report / applied data analytics portfolio project. Not peer reviewed.

## Abstract

This applied analytics project develops an end-to-end framework for identifying telecom customers with elevated churn risk and translating risk estimates into transparent retention decisions. Using 7,043 fictional customer records, it combines data-quality controls, leakage governance, cohort analysis, statistical validation, interpretable classification, probability calibration, threshold selection, customer-level explanations, and scenario-based campaign economics. The observed churn rate was 26.54%. A leakage-safe logistic-regression pipeline achieved a holdout ROC-AUC of 0.8985 and recall of 0.8316 at the selected 0.32 threshold. A prescriptive layer then ranked active customers and interventions under budget and capacity constraints. Under stated base assumptions, the analysis estimated 65.92 customers saved and net benefit of $18,775. These values are planning scenarios rather than observed causal impact. The report shows how predictive risk can be connected to operational retention decisions while clearly separating association, prediction, and causation.

This independent technical report presents an end-to-end telecom churn decision-support project. It moves beyond churn prediction by combining leakage-safe modelling, statistical validation, explainable customer risk, threshold selection, and a budget-constrained retention Decision Centre. The paper includes a reproducible methodology, validated findings, limitations, and links to the live interactive application and source repository.
